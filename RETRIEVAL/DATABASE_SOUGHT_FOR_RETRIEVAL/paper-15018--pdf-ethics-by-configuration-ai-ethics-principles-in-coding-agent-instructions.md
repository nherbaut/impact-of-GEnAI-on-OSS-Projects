  title: '[PDF] Ethics by Configuration: AI Ethics Principles in Coding Agent Instructions'
  authors: Haoyu Gao, Peerachai Banyongrakkul, Marc Cheong, Christoph Treude, Sebastian Baltes, Mansooreh Zahedi
  abstract: As AI coding agents become integral to software development, projects increasingly utilise configuration files,
    such as context files and skills, to govern agent behaviour. While AI ethics principles are widely used in research and
    policy, little is known about whether and how they are reflected when developers configure coding agents. We studied ethics
    principles in 40,079 configuration files across 8,893 GitHub repositories. Using a validated large language model (LLM)
    classification, we measured the prevalence of ethics-related statements, and qualitatively analysed 702 stratified samples
    across 679 files to uncover the ethical considerations reflected in these files and the parties they concern. Ethics-related
    statements appeared in 39.4% of files and 61.5% of repositories; concentrated on accountability, transparency, privacy
    protection, and reliability; and occurred more often in task-specific files such as skills than in project-wide context
    files. Our qualitative analysis identified 30 categories of ethical considerations, which take the form of concrete rules
    for the agent. Accountability appeared mainly as requirements for human approval, transparency as explain and honestly
    report the agent's work, and fairness as accessibility and localisation. These considerations reveal a difference in focus.
    Whereas public ethics frameworks aim to protect society and end users, configuration files mainly concern developers,
    maintainers, and community members in the development workflow. Ethics-related content appeared mainly in prose-based
    files, and whether agents follow these instructions remains untested. We discuss the implications for practitioners, researchers,
    and policymakers.
