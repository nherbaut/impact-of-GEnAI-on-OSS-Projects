  title: '[PDF] Toward an Evidence-Based Template for Agent Instruction Files: An Empirical Study of Adoption, Consistency,

  authors: Mehedi Hasan Shanto, Md. Anaytul Islam, Muhammad Asaduzzaman, Alioune Ngom
  abstract: 'AI coding agents rely on repository-level instruction files to understand project conventions, development workflows,
    and constraints. However, different agents support different instruction formats, and limited empirical evidence exists
    about how these files are adopted, what information they contain, whether coexisting formats provide consistent guidance,
    and how they are maintained. We conduct an empirical study of 3,387 instruction files from 1,000 GitHub repositories across
    four formats: AGENTS.md, CLAUDE.md, GitHub Copilot instructions, and Cursor rules. We find that 42.2% of instruction-bearing
    repositories in the higher-popularity cohort maintain multiple formats. Among 386 manually examined pairs of corresponding
    sections, 16.1% provide divergent guidance. Our maintenance analysis further shows that 40.4% of the analyzed files have
    only one recorded modification event and that maintenance patterns differ across formats. Based on category prevalence
    and category addition ratios (CAR), we derive an evidence-based template containing ten Required, four Gap, and two Recommended
    categories. In an exploratory generation experiment, explicit template guidance increases structural category coverage
    from 18.7% to 82.8%. We further introduce AGIT-Instruct, a benchmark for evaluating instruction-file generation through
    structural coverage and content alignment. Across four generation configurations, retrieval-augmented generation achieves
    the highest full-file ROUGE-1 F1 of 0.459. A manual assessment of 278 generated category instances finds that 19.4% fully
    preserve and 61.2% partially preserve developer-written intent. Our findings provide empirical guidance for organizing
    and maintaining agent instruction'
